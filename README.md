# Hyperwood

The Hyperwood site. Three pages, and a try of a fourth:

- **The landing page** (`index.html`): an opening, then a pinned scroll journey drawn by one shader, with three short films in its hero, then what Hyperwood does on the same Ink: the app in three short films, the markets with a live chart, the mobile app, the network (see "After the journey" below).
- **The Architecture page** (`architecture/index.html`): the long read. Eight sections that follow the architecture paper ("Hyperwood: Architecture and Core Execution") page by page.
- **The earlier landing page** (`classic.html`): the globe hero and the four frames, kept as they were until the new landing page is approved. Not linked from anywhere.
- **The film page** (`film.html`): the first try of the films, on a screen of their own in the manner of bulk.trade's first screen (see "The films, and the film page" below). Not linked from anywhere. The films have since gone into the journey's hero.

The landing page says each thing once and shows it. The Architecture page carries everything the paper says.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev          # http://localhost:5210 and http://localhost:5210/architecture/
npm run build        # type-check and production build (both pages)
npm run lint
npm run test:choreography   # the hero's motion maths: 77 pieces, 9 rings, restored after a scroll there and back
npx tsx scripts/liquid-shape.ts   # how round and even the hero's fused pieces are
npx tsx scripts/check-shader.ts   # the journey's shader, with the mark as its hero, with the room, and with the room and the exchange, is still the committed one, character for character
npx tsx scripts/film/light.ts     # where each film's bulb hangs and how its light falls, beside what the page draws (needs ffmpeg)
node scripts/product/fetch-market.cjs   # the prices the app shows (src/product/market.json), from Yahoo's chart feed
```

The pictures of the app on the landing page are taken of the app's own markup on the studio page (`studio.html`, not linked; what it shows is listed at the top of `src/product/Studio.tsx`): `scripts/product/shots.js` takes them from the dev server, then `bake.py` and `bake-standing.py` make them into `src/assets/product/` (how to run each is at its top). Made again from the same markup, they come out the same to the pixel.

- `?hero=mark` shows the journey with the hero it had before the films: the mark as a body of black liquid.
- `?drop=lens` shows the drop of light with the films' texture (see "The drop through the lens" below), for comparison with the plain drop the page shows.
- `?demo=1` plays the hero's whole scroll as a timed take, with no scrolling.
- `?demo=1&capture=1` hands the clocks to a script: `window.__hyperwoodCapture.step()` renders exact 1/60 s frames, for recording.
- `?debug` (dev server only) reads shader logs back, which makes the page wait for every program.

## The landing page

### The opening

What is on screen while the page gets ready, and how the page is uncovered. The mark and its figure are the same on the landing page and on the earlier one; what it waits for differs, and how the cover goes.

- **It is in the page itself.** Its elements and styles are in `index.html`, so the page is Ink and the Hyperwood mark is already filling with the first paint, before any script or stylesheet has arrived. (The production build links its stylesheets at the end of the body for the same reason: `vite.config.ts`.)
- **The mark fills from the left, in one sweep** (`config.opening.flow`, `finish: 'run'` in `src/ui/opening.ts`). A first movement starts with the first paint, brisk and then creeping for as long as the page takes to arrive. When the hero is whole, that movement is stopped where it stands and one movement runs on from there to the full mark: it leaves at the speed the first had at that moment and eases out, so nothing changes pace where the one takes over. On a quick load the mark is about a fifth full when the page is whole and full a little over half a second later, at an even pace. It is the kit's Flow reveal: a left-to-right wipe that follows the shape. (Until 2026-11-18 it filled as the earlier page's still does, in three movements that add up, with a figure under it that counted to 100. On a quick load the first movement had crept to a tenth or a fifth of the mark when the last one closed all the rest in under half a second: it read as a stall and then a rush. The figure is gone from this page.)
- **Then the cover goes.** On the landing page the mark and the Ink fade, and the tagline comes into focus where the mark stood (`journey.intro`): nothing travels over the screen. Until 2026-11-17 the Ink sank and a wave ran down the screen as its lit edge; that was taken out at the client's word. The earlier page (`classic.html`) still has it: its cover asks for the wave with `data-crest`, and its own styles sink it.
- **Nothing in it is moved frame by frame.** Each movement is handed to the browser whole, as an animation of transform (`src/ui/opening.ts`), and the browser runs those on its own thread. While the page is busy starting up, and it is for a tenth of a second at a time, the mark keeps filling evenly. (On the earlier page the figure is one column of numbers stepped the same way.)
- **It decides when the page may show.** On the landing page: the journey's program is compiled and the typeface has arrived (`journeyOpening` in `src/journey/timeline.ts`; with the mark as hero, the mark's fields are worked out too), and never later than 4 s. The films are not waited for: they are asked for once the page has been let go, and their first frames stand in as stills until they play. On the earlier page: both mountains are in the picture, a short check has held the story's heaviest moment behind the cover and, on a slow machine, stepped the drawing down a size so scrolling stays smooth, and frames are even (`config.opening`).
- On the test machine the landing page's mark is full about 0.8 s after the page is requested (production build, local server), the page is uncovered at about 1 s, and the cover has gone half a second later. The earlier page takes about 1.8 s. The page cannot be scrolled until then.
- **The journey's program is kept with its canvas** (`built` in `src/journey/timeline.ts`). In development React stops and starts every effect once as the page mounts. The journey used to throw its program away and ask for it again there, and throwing away a program that is still being built makes the graphics process wait for the build: the whole screen stood still for a quarter of a second with the mark part filled (measured: one 243 ms task on the graphics process, no frame shown meanwhile). A journey started again on the same canvas now takes the program over. The built site never did this, since effects run once there; the development page is where the work is looked at.
- Reduced motion: the mark is simply there, whole, and the cover fades.
- `performance.mark` entries named `hw:...` record how a start-up went. The landing page: mark-there, journey-program, scene, journey-whole, ready, journey-first-frame. The earlier page: scene, programs-asked, programs-there, first-frame, mountain-there, check-heavy, check-done, ready. The browser's performance tools show them.

### The journey

The top of the page is one pinned stage, held for about nine screens of scrolling, while one shader over the whole screen tells the story (`src/journey/shader.ts`). One number drives it: how far the section has been scrolled (`timeline.ts`). When each thing happens is `journey.beats`, `journey.lids` and `journey.room` in `src/config.ts`, as `src/journey/timing.ts` carries them into the journey the page shows; the page and the shader both read them there.

**The hero before the films is kept whole.** Until 2026-11-18 the hero was the mark: the drop ran out into the Hyperwood mark (the kit's own morph), the mark's light drew back to its edges and it was a body of black liquid that turned towards the pointer, and it gathered into the drop again. `?hero=mark` shows that journey, at the length it had (six and a half screens), with the wordmark and the approved line under the body and the dotted matrix round the pointer. Its parts are the `MARK_` pieces of `shader.ts` and `markField.ts`, and `npx tsx scripts/check-shader.ts` says whether its shader is still, character for character, the committed one. The points below that speak of the mark as a body describe it.

How it is made:

- **The hero is a room** (`config.journey.room`, the `ROOM_` pieces of `shader.ts`, `src/film/FilmStack.tsx`). The films are video files in a box that lies under the canvas. For this hero the canvas keeps its alpha, and the shader leaves a share of each point clear: where the lamp's light has reached, the film shows through, and everywhere else the picture is what it would be without the films. So the lids and the light are drawn over the films by the one shader, and nothing has to be masked in the page as the light moves.
- **The journey's light is the films' bulb.** Each film has one bare bulb, finished in Hyper Neon. The page knows where each bulb hangs (`bulb` in `src/film/reel.ts`) and how its light falls off with distance (`room.light`); both are measured on the finished films, and `npx tsx scripts/film/light.ts` prints them beside what the page draws. The drop goes to the bulb of the film that is showing and is made into it in two steps that run into one another: its size and its edge become the bulb's, the edge ever softer, then its fall is the bulb's own. By then the light the shader draws and the bulb in the film are one picture, so the hand-over shows only as the room appearing around the lamp. Leaving is the same backwards. (A plain mix of the crisp drop and the soft lamp was tried first: half way it is a dim disc with a hot middle, two tones where there should be one light.)
- **The light opens the room.** How far the lamp's light reaches is one number (`room.open`, `room.shut`). Its front is soft by a share of how far it has got (`room.soft`), so no edge travels, and with the light all the way out the whole box is the film's.
- **The room's edges melt into the page.** Above and below, the box itself fades out, plainly (`filmStack.module.css`). At either side the shader lets the film give way to Ink (`room.side`). A box that runs from edge to edge of the screen, as on a phone, keeps its sides: the screen's edges are its own. (The film page smears its films along the upper and lower edge, by drawing them a second time under a blur. Here that second copy cost frames: see "Measured".)
- **Three films, one after another as the page is scrolled** (`room.turns`, `room.turn`, `timing.ts`). A change has three parts: the light draws back to the lamp; the lamp, alone in the dark, goes over to where the next film's bulb hangs, and in the middle of that dark the page puts the next film in the box; the light opens the next room. It is the old films' own way from one scene to the next: the iris closes on the one and opens on the other. The film that is in the box plays, round and round (the films are finished so that their end runs on into their beginning); the others rest.
- **On the films' screens there is nothing but the films, their words and the call to action** at the upper right (`Header.tsx`): no logo, no way around the site. Those belong to the gate.
- **The gate is laid out as the reference lays out its first screen** (bulk.trade, the reference named for it: `journey.module.css`, from "The closing as the page's hero"). The logo at the upper left. A column at the right, a third of the screen: the way around the site between two hairlines, the gate's line, the call to action. The tagline at the gate's left, set as the reference sets its own: two lines, the first in 800 and the second in 400 and set in, at a leading of 0.8 and a tracking of -0.03em (the reference's measures, read off its page). The second line is set in so that it ends where the first ends, by no more than the reference's 0.95 of the type size (`--tag-indent`, found by the page), so the tagline is no wider than its first line and can be as large as the room allows. Its capitals begin level with the gate's crown. It is as large as the reference's tagline (the reference's own measure: 7.55 hundredths of the width up to 1440 px and growing faster above, 109 px at 1440 by 900 and 162 px at 1905), and never so large that it comes near the gate: the page measures how wide its widest line is in ems once the typeface is there (`Journey.tsx`), and its size is at most the room beside the gate over that. The room ends 0.03 of the picture's height short of the gate, where its glow begins. "Trade the market. / Privately, anytime." is 70 px at 1440 by 900, 94 px at 1905 by 949 and 126 px at 2560 by 1440: its lines are 8.2 of its size wide, and in two lines that is as large as the room beside the gate allows (the same words in four lines were 120 px at 1905). At a leading of 0.8 a letter hanging below one line can meet one of the next: "Trade the market." has none, other words need their letters checked. The words are `gate` in `src/content.home.ts`, a draft for approval. The logo is the lockup at the kit's size for a navigation bar, 160 px wide, with its word's baseline on the baseline of the panel's first row (the lockup itself: "Brand"). The column's measures are the reference's, taken at 1440 by 900 and four other sizes, and above 1440 px the whole arrangement grows with the width (the logo with it). On a screen with no room for the column (under 1024 px, upright, or a phone on its side) the logo stands level with the call to action in the header; on an upright screen the tagline stands across the top, above the gate, each sentence in its two halves, four lines (59 px on a phone 390 px wide, where two lines would be 42 px), as wide as the screen allows and never so tall that it comes within 24 px of the gate's crown. There the two light halves stand 0.07 of the type size further apart, or the y of "Privately," comes down onto the e of "anytime.".
- **Each film has its words** (`films` in `src/content.home.ts`): a title across the room's lower edge, reaching over it by 0.3 of its size, and a smaller sentence under it, the size of the reference's line. The title is as large as the reference's tagline where it fits (162 px at 1905 by 949, 109 px at 1440 by 900): never wider than the screen, and never so large that it and its sentence do not fit under the film. The page measures the titles once the typeface is there (`Journey.tsx`), so other words take their own size. On an upright screen each title stands in its two halves, one under the other (76 px on a phone 390 px wide). They come into focus as their room opens and go out of focus as the light draws back from it. Read in turn the three titles are one story, the story of a trade (what you do, what becomes of it, what settles it), and the gate's tagline ends it. The films are in that order: the board, the booth, the press.
- **The gate's links answer the pointer as the reference's do** (`src/ui/ScrambleLink.tsx`): for a moment every letter is a chance letter or figure, new with every frame, and the word settles from its first letter on, one letter every 85 ms, while a small icon comes beside it: four and four, as the reference has them: the site's parts on the left (Products, Markets, Learn, Stake), each with an arrow after it, and X, Discord, Telegram and Docs on the right, each with its own mark before it (Docs an open book). The icons are the reference's, Phosphor Icons in their bold weight (MIT licensed; `src/ui/icons.tsx`), 1.15 of the type size and a little faint. Products opens the app on this page; Learn and Docs open the Architecture page until there are guides and docs of their own; Stake and the channels have no address yet, and until then those links point nowhere (`panel` in `src/content.home.ts`).
- **The call to action moves as the reference's button does** (`src/ui/LaunchLink.tsx`, read off that page frame by frame): its letters roll up one after another, each in a window one line high over a copy of itself, the next 29 ms after the one before, each in about 450 ms on a steep ease-out; when the pointer leaves they drop back at once, which cannot be seen, since a letter and its copy are the same. Meanwhile the arrow after the words goes out to the right as one comes in before them, on the same curve, and the button turns white. Under reduced motion nothing scrambles, rolls or slides.
- **The drop through the lens** (`?drop=lens`, a try beside the plain drop the page shows). The drop is drawn as the films' lamp is seen through their lens while out of focus: a shade deeper inside than at its rim, with the films' grain and flicker on it and on its glow, at their 24 frames a second. Grain goes both ways, towards Ink and towards the hot Neon kept for highlights, so the light is as bright as it was on the whole. The rim goes as the drop comes into focus as the lamp, the grain stays (the films have it), and all of it goes as the drop rises to the gate. (Faint rings inside the round, as some lenses give, were tried too: moving, they read as a target.)
- **The exchange** (`?gate=exchange`, a try beside the guide's picture, which the page still shows). The gate is the doorway of a stock exchange of our own: a temple front of six fluted columns under a plain pediment, on a podium with a flight of steps, before a wet cobbled street, at night. Its entablature is in the classical bands: an architrave of three bands, each standing a little further out, a plain frieze, a row of dentils under the corona, whose underside reads as a line of shadow; the pediment's raking cornice is one depth all along, down into the corners, and its tympanum is set back within it. Nothing in it comes from another picture: a small path tracer renders it in the browser (`scripts/exchange/render.js`), into four pictures that the page puts together while it is scrolled (`src/journey/exchange.ts`; `shader.ts`, "the exchange"): the building under a faint night sky, the same lit by the open doorway alone, the same lit by the river alone, and where each point lies under the river (how far along it, how far from its middle). The doorway has the gate's measures (1.603 times as tall as wide), so the drop becomes it as it becomes the gate. As the gate stands, its light reaches out over the steps, the columns and the wet street, and the building comes out of the night with it, black and white; then its light runs down the steps, a little broader at each, its edges clean and each riser under it a shade deeper than the treads, so it runs down the steps and does not lie over them; at their foot the river the page had before takes it on, as broad, opens out to its own breadth as it comes onto the street, as water does at the foot of a flight of steps, and runs away over the street as it did, rippling as it runs: the guide's painted river, laid at the size it had, its doorway's foot at the steps' foot (its doorway is the exchange's, and is left out). The one gives way to the other along a level line at the steps' foot, the guide's river whole before the other goes (ended along its own course, which turns as it leaves the steps, the light on the steps left a slanting seam there). The exchange's own river, baked with its pictures (Neon along its middle, a shade deeper towards its margins), runs down the steps, and on over the street only if the guide's picture does not come. The coffered ceiling over the doorway takes its light, and the doorway glows on the air as the films' bulb glows: a close bright rim and a broad soft fall, so it is the building's light and not laid on it. Everything is smooth: no grain over the pictures, a single render sample is never let outshine the doorway itself (rare bright samples stay as specks however many are taken), the steps are dry stone, and the setts are only a little domed and worn: they turn the light nearly as the level street would, so the doorway's reflection lies on the wet street as one soft pool and not as a spark on every stone (their edges, steep as they are, caught it as a bright dash each). The ground before the steps is wet all over, so the pool begins at the steps' foot. The guide's river is read through a small blur (its painting's grain broke its edges into specks), and with WebGL 2 the three lights are kept as one channel each with their smaller copies, so they are read smoothly where they are drawn smaller than they are. (Tried and taken out: the films' grain and flicker over the pictures, which read as specks; the door leaves standing open beside the doorway, which, seen edge on, read as light leaking round it; a frame set a few centimetres off the opening, which read as a stray line round the arch; setts with rounder edges, whose pattern caught the doorway more, not less; matt dry setts, which took most of the doorway's pool away with the specks.) The pictures are as tall as the screen, or taller where the screen is wider than their 2.1 to 1, the doorway's top 0.2818 of their height down; beside them the tagline has the room up to the building's outer columns. On an upright screen they are only as tall as lets the whole building stand within the screen's width, and they stand low, under the tagline, the river running off the bottom. The four weigh 0.9 MB. A frame of the journey costs about 0.3 ms on the graphics card while the exchange is on the screen (0.16 without it; before the doors begin to open nothing of it is worked out), and the production build showed every frame on time through the doors' opening and the river's pour (traced at 1905 by 949).
- **Two tries beside the exchange**, each behind an address of its own; without it the page is as above (`scripts/check-shader.ts` says so of the shader).

- **Type never lies on the light.** The closing words stand beside the gate's upper part (`--closing-level`, `--door-gap` in `journey.module.css`), above where it opens out into the river, so they are one colour from end to end. The other is the shape a film takes when it is held along the outline and blown up from inside: its root is a round cross-section, as thick as the stroke is wide, so the necks are thin tubes and the lobes full drops, with no crease anywhere. The shader marches a ray through the two. Which way the surface faces is worked out from the shape itself (how its thickness falls away), not from differences of the distance: near the outline those see a thin sheet and call it flat, which showed as a dark seam inside the rim.

It is never bent or distorted, and it is always the one colour: light that falls on it (the pointer's, or a sheen that passes now and then when there is no pointer) shows in the glow around the letters, never in the letters. Three rounds of drawing a river by code here never came out as natural, so this page does as the guide does (`journey.picture`, and the block that begins "the gate and the river" in `shader.ts`). The gate the shader has drawn hands over to the picture (`picture.mix`); one soft front then reveals the picture from the gate's foot downward (`picture.arrive`); below the gate the picture ripples, so the river sways and runs. Those numbers are the guide's own, the ripple's too.
- **The gate's box places the picture.** The picture's doorway is laid on the gate's box (`door` in the layout), and the box has the doorway's measures: its top 30.9 hundredths down the screen, its breadth 0.136 of the picture's height, its foot where the guide's own doorway closes (`journey.module.css`, `picture.arch`). So the gate the shader draws and the picture's doorway are one on any screen. As in the guide, the picture is as tall as the screen, and taller where the screen is wide.
- **Its colours are read as light, as softly as they are painted.** The picture is a soft thing: a pale body (pink, blue, coral, all about as light as one another) whose rims fall away through deep vivid violet into black. It is its lightness that is read, in a straight line (`picture.light`): its body comes out as full Neon and its rims as a glow that falls away into Ink over the same breadth as the picture's own. Where the picture runs cool (the bands along the river's edges) the Neon is a shade deeper (`picture.tone`). Nothing is paler than Neon. A steep reading was tried first (full Neon from a little over half as light): it turned the pour into a flat shape with a hard edge, which swallowed the gate and stood round it all at once. Keep the line straight.
- **The gate stays a gate.** The guide's doorway is orange above and pale below, where its light pours out. In one colour that is a gate whose crown is a shade deeper than its foot and the pour (`picture.crown`), in one even fall by height. The gate the shader draws before the picture has the same fall, so nothing in the gate changes when the pour comes. (Reading the fall off the picture's own orange gave a blotched gate.) Its glow thins out towards its foot.
- **Checked frame by frame, on the light itself.** Frames of the guide's own preview, from the standing doorway to the whole river, are each matched to our frame at the same moment. The reference is read by its true lightness and ours by how much Neon there is, and the door is compared close up, row by row: how far from the door's middle the light falls below a tenth, a quarter, a half and three quarters. At the gate's foot the half-light lies 1.38 gate half-widths out in the original and 1.32 in ours; beside the gate's middle 1.21 and 1.17. (An earlier check read both through the same steep curve, so it could not see that ours was hard where the original is soft.)
- **Where it leaves the guide.** The words stand beside the gate (above). Before the picture, the gate is our own: the mark gathers into it. The guide lets the whole of the gate's skirt in at once, faint and then brighter; here the picture takes over at the gate's foot first and a little later the further from the foot a point lies (`picture.out`), so the light shows under the foot and spreads into the bell. Each point still comes in as softly as the next, so no edge travels. And on a wide screen the guide's ripple reaches a little past the picture's sides in the bottom corners, where the guide shows black (a notch in the river): here the picture's last three hundredths are drawn out smoothly (`picture.margin`).
- **If the picture does not come** (the page asks for it where the guide keeps it, `picture.url`), the traced wave is drawn in its place, with its own gate and words. To calm or still the river, lower the ripple's numbers in the shader; to use another picture, keep its doorway where this one's is.
- **The two earlier currents are still there**, drawn by the shader itself. `?wave=traced`: the wave of the reference frame, traced (`layWave`, `journey.wave`): nineteen points pull at a cubic B-spline, which is cut into 64 pieces whose breadth runs evenly from end to end, and the shader adds a soft band for every piece; its edge is drawn by how far inside the outline a point lies, and the gate opens into it like a bell. `?wave=old`: the first course (`layCourse`, `journey.floor`), two sweeps on a floor in perspective.
- **What was tried before, and why it went.** A current measured against its nearest reach has a seam wherever another reach becomes the nearest. A soft band laid on the floor, not on the screen, smears towards the viewer. A current in one flat colour with a crisp edge is no wave at all. A course drawn through fitted points has necks and bumps; a smooth curve cured those, but where the gate meets the current a sum of soft strokes still makes a ball, not a bell. The picture has none of these troubles: it was painted whole.
- **The lids are shared.** Their numbers (`journey.lids`) are read by the shader and by the script that tints the words, so the words change exactly where the lids are.
- **The wheel glides** (`glide.ts`): a wheel moves a goal and the page's real scroll follows it. Touch, the keyboard and the scrollbar are the browser's own.
- **Without WebGL** nothing is drawn: the stage stays Ink, the room comes and goes with the hero's words and goes dark between two films (its sides faded by the page; the earlier hero shows the symbol and the wordmark as they are supplied), the gate is a plain shape of light, and the words keep a closer timing so the stage is never empty (`journey.stillBeats`). **Reduced motion:** no ambient movement, no easing, no glide; the scroll still tells the story.
- **Screens.** Checked in a desktop browser at 1440 by 900, 1920 by 1080 and 1280 by 720, and emulated at 820 by 1180, 390 by 844, 360 by 640, and a phone on its side (844 by 390, 667 by 375). The room was checked at the same sizes but the last. Not yet on a real phone.

What is there:

- **The app, in three films** (`src/landing/reel/`). Three columns, the outer two a step lower. Each is a claim, a short text and a film of the app doing it: "Long or short." **Three films** (`src/assets/film/`, each 960 by 720, 7.3 seconds, about 1.8 MB, with its first frame as a still), one for each step of the story the landing page tells with them:
- `booth`: a broker alone in a lit telephone booth, writing down an order, while the floor of a 1920s stock exchange crowds past outside the glass. Private, in the middle of the market.
- `board`: a board boy on a ladder chalking prices onto a great blackboard, above a crowd of brokers shouting and waving order slips. Long or short, on what the board says.
- `seal`: a settlement clerk pressing the seal into stock certificates at an iron press, while clerks and runners hurry past his desk. The proof that settles it.

How they are made:
- **Each has one still figure at work, an uproar around it, and one bare bulb.** The bulb is Hyper Neon, the one coloured thing in the picture and the same in all three: the brand's light in each scene. On an upright screen, which shows a slice of a film, each film shows the slice with the figure in it and the bulb clear of the card (`at` in `reel.ts`).
- **They are generated footage:** a still from a text prompt, then eight seconds of image-to-video from that still at 720p, the camera locked off (the bulb must not move: its light is coloured where it stands). Made on Higgsfield, the stills by Nano Banana 2, the films by Seedance 2.5. The stills were corrected before they were filmed: the blackboard carries numbers only (a generated board came with real companies' names and garbled words), and nothing covers the clerk's eyes. They show no real person, place or event, and stand for none: they are art direction.
- **Their finish** is `scripts/film/finish.sh` (ffmpeg and node; the three calls are in its header), and it gives the same file each time. A generated clip comes sharp and clean; the reference's films are soft, smeared, haloed and worn, and the finish takes ours there. In black and white: deeper blacks; the room falling away into the dark around the figure, so that the figure is the subject; whatever moves smeared over seven frames, as by a long exposure, while what stands still stays as it is; a soft focus; halation; grain; a slight flicker. A film goes round: the take's last three quarters of a second dissolve into its first (`LOOP`), so nothing jumps where it starts again; the camera is locked, so only what moves dissolves. Then colour (`tint.cjs`): the picture's black is the page's Ink, so a film has no box where it is dark; the light of the bulb, and only that, is Hyper Neon; and the film gets thin scratches and dust.
- **The bulb's light.** Each pixel near the bulb is read as the bulb's glow plus whatever else is there. The glow takes the straight Ink to Neon mix and the rest stays neutral, so the glass and its halo are Neon and a face or a collar beside it keeps its own white. How the glow falls off is measured on each clip, and it is never wider than the booth film's: before a pale wall the wall's own grey reads as glow, and the Neon would spread over it as a haze. The bulb's glass decodes to about 203, 253, 0 (Neon is 204, 255, 0) and a dark corner to 10, 10, 10.

### The hero

One continuous WebGL scene, pinned for 9.5 screens of scroll.

A drop of black liquid the size of a planet hangs over a dark valley, in front of a Hyper Neon corona. Scrolling follows one order through the stages the protocol names: the core starts to glow through the glass (Accepted), the drop tears open into turning bands with light pouring between them (Matched), the bands burst into droplets that sweep past the camera (Proven), and the last droplet settles under a neon aurora and becomes the Hyperwood mark (Settled). Scrolling back rebuilds the drop exactly.

**How the liquid is drawn.** Nothing in the globe is a triangle mesh. At rest it is one analytic sphere with a slow swell, which a mirror-like material needs to stay perfectly smooth. Once the scroll begins it hands over to 77 soft ellipsoids fused by a smooth union: pieces that touch run into one surface, pieces that drift apart neck and pinch off like droplets. Each pixel ray-marches that field, so the surface has no facets at any zoom. Details are in the header of `src/scene/liquid/liquidMaterial.ts`.

**The ending: the drop draws the mark.** The brand kit names one way to connect a circle and the logo (kit 03.9, Logo animation): a drop becomes the mark. The hero ends on that, kept small and quiet. The drop is the mark's own first lobe, and it draws the rest.

- **The drop gathers light.** As the sky darkens, the last droplet settles into a still sphere and neon closes in from its rim, driven by the scroll, so the eye never loses it.
- **Then it draws the mark.** The drop settles exactly where the mark's first lobe will be (`mark.start`). When the story reaches its last stretch it finishes filling, then pours out along the mark: up the first wave, down, over the second, and round into the head. This part runs on the clock, not on the scroll, takes `mark.draw` seconds, and runs back when the scroll goes back.
- **How the drawing is made** (`src/brand/flow.ts`). For every point inside the master, one number is worked out once: when the liquid gets there if it starts as the drop and can only run inside the outline. It is a travel time, solved outward from the drop with the fast marching method, and the liquid runs fastest mid-stream and slower along the walls (`mark.wall`, `mark.reach`). The shader shows the points whose time has come, and lets the height fall to nothing at the front (`mark.nose`) so the tip is lit like the tip of a stream.
- **The kit morph is still there.** Set `mark.reveal` to `'morph'`, or add `?mark=morph` to the address.
- **It is liquid.** A blurred copy of the shape is its height, and it is lit from that: a soft key, a deeper rim, a glint, and the cursor light sliding over it (`mark.depth`).
- **Where it departs from the kit.** The kit reveals the logo with a morph in 900 ms, rules out drawing it along its path, and keeps it free of effects. Here the drop draws the mark over a few seconds (asked for), the colour is the graded neon of the scene, and there is a soft halo behind the mark.

**Screens.** The scene is composed for a wide screen and adapts to tall ones (phones, upright tablets): the lens widens so the whole globe fits the width, the camera aims lower so the globe sits above the copy, and the last droplet takes a larger share of the width (`camera.tall`, `phrase.keyScreenWidthTall`). On those screens the header and the copy sit on a soft Ink shade.

## Brand

Colours, type and the lockup come from the Hyperwood brand kit (`hbrand`): Hyper Neon `#CCFF00`, Ink `#0A0A0A`, Manrope, IBM Plex Mono, the horizontal lockup. The lockup is built, not taken as supplied: the kit's lockup files carry a wordmark traced from a picture, whose letters wobble and are unevenly spaced (the w touches the o). `src/assets/brand/hyperwood-lockup-neon.svg` keeps the kit's symbol as it is and outlines the word from the kit's typeface, Manrope ExtraBold, with the font's own kerning and the letters 0.02 em closer, placed as the kit places its word (capitals 0.71 of the lockup's height, the first letter 0.49 of the symbol's height clear of it). To build it again: `python scripts/brand/lockup.py <the hbrand folder>` (fontTools and uharfbuzz). The kit's own files still carry the trace. The liquid follows the kit's image direction: broad continuous surfaces, glossy dark material, neon reflected light, quiet space.

**The picture uses two colours only.** The kit colours its liquid field as a straight mix of Ink and Hyper Neon, so the last step of the hero's picture does the same: the brightness of each pixel picks its place on that line (`config.post.brand`, the shader is in `src/scene/PostFX.tsx`). Light keeps its shape but cannot turn pale or white, and the brightest thing on screen is exactly `#CCFF00`. White stays for type, which is drawn outside the scene. The deck's liquid ends on the same line. Add `?brand=0` to the address to see the hero without the grade.

## Keeping it smooth

Measured on a 1920 by 1080, 120 Hz screen (RTX 5060).

- **The hero.** A frame costs about 2.5 ms for most of the story and about 5 ms while the bands are open, against a budget of 8.3 ms. The liquid shader keeps almost no per-pixel state: large per-pixel tables are very slow under Direct3D (the backend WebGL uses on Windows) for every pixel on screen. Do not add arrays to that shader without measuring. Rays that skim a surface take growing steps. The drawing buffer is capped at `config.performance.maxPixels`.
- **Starting up: nothing may stop the screen.** The opening is animated by the browser's own thread, but that thread shares the graphics process with everything else, so anything that makes the graphics process wait freezes the opening too. Four things did, and are arranged so they no longer do:
  - *Programs are compiled beside the page, not in a drawn frame* (`src/scene/warm.ts`). Before the first frame the scene, the post chain and the two small scenes drawn beside them are asked for, and nothing is drawn until the driver says they are done. A program depends on where it is drawn and on which lights are on, so they are asked for in that state: with a linear buffer in place (the composer draws into one), and once for every set of the two lights the story switches. The mountains arrive later, from a worker, and are shown only when their programs are there.
  - *The payout frame's canvas is made late* (`src/deck/Payout.tsx`): when its frame is a few screens away, and its program is never waited for.
  - *The frames below the hero are not drawn while the opening is up* (`deck.module.css`). For that first moment the page is short and they sit right under the screen.
  - *The terrain worker starts with the page* (`src/main.tsx`), not with the hero's code, so the two overlap.
- **How to check it.** Record a trace of a page load with the browser's performance tools (or Chrome tracing with the `viz`, `gpu` and `toplevel` categories). The frames put on screen while the opening is up should come at the display's own pace: on the test machine a first visit loses 4 or 5 single frames in two seconds and never two in a row for long (the worst gap is 25 ms). A long task on the graphics process's main thread, or a program status read that waits, is what to look for when that changes.
- **The journey's first picture waits for a still screen** (`mayBegin` in `src/journey/timeline.ts`). Compiling the program beside the page is not the whole cost: the first time a program draws, the graphics driver builds it, and with this shader that stopped every animation on screen for about 36 ms, the opening's among them. So while the opening is up the program is compiled and the mark's fields are worked out, but nothing is handed to the driver. The first picture is drawn once the opening words stand still, about half a second after the cover has gone, or at the first scroll. Nothing is lost by it: the first screen is Ink, which the stage is anyway. On the test machine a first visit now shows no late frame from the graphics process while the opening is up, and one (20 ms) at that first picture.
- **The deck.** One listener and one frame loop; pictures draw only when something changed. The hero's scene stops drawing once the first frame covers it.

To measure the hero: the dev build exposes `window.__hyperwood`; render with `gl.render(scene, camera)` in a loop and force the GPU to finish with a one-pixel `readPixels`, which gives the true cost without the display refresh in the way.

Checked in a desktop browser at 1440 by 900, 1920 by 1080, 1366 by 650 and at phone width. Not yet checked on a real phone, where the browser toolbars change the visible height.

### The exchange: rendering it again

The scene and its camera are `scripts/exchange/render.js`, in metres: the doorway's breadth (`door`), the columns' spacing (`mid`, `axis`), the camera (`eye`, `dist`, `tan`, `shift`), and the river's course in plan (`COURSE`). On the dev server open `/scripts/exchange/render.html?w=3150&h=1500&spp=800`. When `window.__done` is true, `window.__show('light')` and `window.__show('space')` draw the two outputs on the canvas: save each at its own pixels. `window.__geometry` says where the doorway and the steps' risers lie, for `src/journey/exchange.ts` and the `--door-*` measures in `journey.module.css`. Then, into `src/assets/exchange/`:

```sh
ffmpeg -i light.png -vf extractplanes=r,hqdn3d=1.5:1.5:0:0 -q:v 3 base.jpg   # g for door.jpg (hqdn3d 2), b for glow.jpg (hqdn3d 3, -q:v 4)
ffmpeg -i space.png -vf scale=1575:750:flags=area -pix_fmt rgb24 space.png
```

The pictures in the page took 800 samples at that size, about 3.5 minutes on a desktop graphics card; `mode=preview` shows the whole put together roughly, to look at.
